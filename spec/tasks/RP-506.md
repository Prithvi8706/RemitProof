## 1. Objective and rationale

Show, before the solver decides anything, that it returns exactly the allocations three unrelated searches return:

1. the existing enumerator, `find_valid_alternatives`;
2. the brute force of RP-106 (`oracle` in `backend/tests/test_search_properties.py`), integer cents and bit masks;
3. OR-Tools CP-SAT, through a new test helper that builds its model from the records.

Why three: the enumerator is the behaviour to preserve, and it stops at 16 invoices and 3 credits. The brute force is independent and stops near 12 invoices. CP-SAT is independent, has no such limit, and is the only reference for ledgers the other two cannot reach (DD-16).

The task also settles, by known-answer tests, what CP-SAT's statuses mean. That was not taken from documentation. Observed in the dry run:

| Status | Number | Meaning established by a known answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `INFEASIBLE` | 3 | Proven: no solution, or no further solution after one was excluded |
| `OPTIMAL` | 4 | A solution was found. With enumeration switched on: every solution was found. |
| `FEASIBLE` | 2 | An enumeration was cut off by a limit after at least one solution |
| `UNKNOWN` | 0 | Cut off by a limit with nothing proven. Observed on an instance that has a solution. |

The consequence for the oracle: uniqueness is claimed only when the second solve returns `INFEASIBLE`. `UNKNOWN` never counts as "no solution".

Finally, four adversarial ledger families are added, each built to defeat one kind of naive pruning. Section 10 lists which injected defect each family caught.

The plan had this task at 3 hours. It is 5: the oracle is a module of its own, and the known-answer tests for CP-SAT were not in the scope statement.

## 6. Input and output contracts

### 6.1 `new_solver(deterministic_limit)`

A `CpSolver` with `parameters.num_workers = 1` and `parameters.random_seed = 0`. If `deterministic_limit` is given, `parameters.max_deterministic_time` is set to it. The limit is CP-SAT's deterministic time, not wall-clock time, so that a cut-off does not depend on how busy the machine is.

### 6.2 The model, one per customer

Amounts are converted with `int(amount * 100)`. For each customer ID that owns at least one given invoice, ascending:

1. Open invoices: that customer's invoices with status `open`, no `allocated_payment_id`, the payment currency, and `issue_date <= payment.date`. A customer with none gets no model.
2. Usable credits: credits whose own `customer_id` is that customer, with status `valid`, no `consumed_by_payment_id`, the payment currency, and an `invoice_id` that is one of the open invoices.
3. One Boolean variable per open invoice ("taken") and one per usable credit ("used").
4. For each open invoice that has at least one usable credit: the cents of its used credits are at most the invoice cents times its "taken" variable. Because every credit amount is positive, this one constraint says both "a credit needs its invoice" and "credits may not exceed the invoice".
5. Cents of the taken invoices minus cents of the used credits equals the payment cents.
6. At least one invoice is taken.

### 6.3 `cpsat_enumerate`

For each model: a solver from `new_solver(deterministic_limit)` with `parameters.enumerate_all_solutions = True` and a solution callback that records `(customer_id, frozenset of taken invoice IDs, frozenset of used credit IDs)`.

Returns `(complete, keys)`. `keys` is the union over all customers. `complete` is `True` exactly when every solve returned `OPTIMAL` or `INFEASIBLE`. `MODEL_INVALID` raises `AssertionError("CP-SAT rejected the model")`.

### 6.4 `cpsat_two_solve`

For each model, with a fresh solver per solve:

1. Solve. `INFEASIBLE`: this customer has no allocation, go on. `UNKNOWN`: remember that a solve was cut off, go on. `MODEL_INVALID`: raise as above. Anything else: one allocation found.
2. Add one clause that forbids exactly the assignment found: for every "taken" and "used" variable, the variable if it was 0 and its negation if it was 1. Solve again. `INFEASIBLE`: no second allocation for this customer. `UNKNOWN`: remember the cut-off. Anything else: a second allocation found.

Result: `ambiguous` if two or more allocations were found in total, whatever was cut off. Otherwise `unknown` if any solve was cut off. Otherwise `unique` for one and `none` for zero.

### 6.5 Helpers in the test file

- `_money(cents)`: `Decimal(f"{cents // 100}.{cents % 100:02d}")`.
- `_invoice(invoice_id, cents, customer_id="CUS_1", **overrides)`, `_credit(credit_id, invoice_id, cents, customer_id="CUS_1", **overrides)`: the defaults of the helpers of the same names in `backend/tests/test_search.py`, with the amount given in cents.
- `_bundle(target_cents, invoices, credits=())`: payment `PAY_1` of that amount dated 2026-08-10 from "Acme Trading Ltd", bank reference "BNK-1"; customers `[ACME, BOREALIS]`; no emails.
- `_as_keys(solutions)`: solver solutions as a set of `Key`.
- `_solver_keys(bundle)`: `solve(build_problem(bundle, CREDIT_LIMIT))`; asserts status `complete` and that no solution occurs twice; returns the keys.
- `_enumerator_keys(bundle)`, `_brute_keys(bundle)` (the RP-106 `oracle` on the bundle's payment, invoices and credits), `_cpsat_keys(bundle)` (asserts `complete`).
- `_verdict(count)`: `none`, `unique` or `ambiguous` for 0, 1 or more.
- `_draw(key, low, high)`: `low + int(hashlib.sha256(key.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest(), 16) % (high - low + 1)`.
- `_subset_sum(amounts, target)`: a bare CP-SAT model with one Boolean per amount, the weighted sum equal to the target, and at least one variable true. Returns `(model, variables)`.
- `_Count`: a solution callback that counts.

## 8. Algorithm

### 8.1 `_large_ledger(seed)`: ledgers beyond the enumerator's limits

For `customer` in `("CUS_1", "CUS_2")`, with `tag` the last character of the customer ID:

1. `count = _draw(f"large:{seed}:{tag}:count", 17, 20)`.
2. `priced = _draw(f"large:{seed}:{tag}:shape", 0, 2) == 0`.
3. For `index` in `range(count)` with `key = f"large:{seed}:{tag}:{index}"`: the amount in cents is `2500 * _draw(key, 1, 400)` if `priced`, else `_draw(key, 5000, 2000000)`. The invoice ID is `f"INV_{tag}{index:02d}"`.
4. Credits: `_draw(f"large:{seed}:{tag}:credits", 0, 6)` of them. For credit `index` with `key = f"large:{seed}:{tag}:credit:{index}"`: it is linked to the invoice at position `_draw(key + ":invoice", 0, min(2, count - 1))` of this customer's invoice IDs in ascending order, and its amount is `max(1, invoice cents * _draw(key + ":share", 1, 60) // 100)`. Its ID is `f"CR_{tag}{index}"`.

The payment. Take the invoices of `CUS_1`, sort them by `hashlib.sha256(f"large:{seed}:paid:{invoice_id}".encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()`, and keep the first `_draw(f"large:{seed}:paid", 1, 5)`. The target starts as the sum of their cents. Then, for every credit in creation order (both customers) that is linked to a kept invoice and has `_draw(f"large:{seed}:apply:{credit_id}", 0, 1) == 1`: if the credits already applied to that invoice plus this one do not exceed the invoice, and the target minus this credit is at least 1, apply it and subtract it from the target.

So every ledger has at least one allocation by construction, 17 to 20 invoices per customer (the enumerator stops at 16), and up to six credits on three invoices (the enumerator never combines more than three).

### 8.2 The four families

Each function returns a list of `(label, bundle)`. All invoices are of `CUS_1` unless stated.

**`equal_amounts`.** For `count` in `(6, 9, 12)`: invoices `INV_00` to `INV_<count-1>` (two digits) of 250000 cents. Five payments: 250000; `250000 * (count // 2)`; `250000 * count`; `250000 * (count // 2) + 1`; `250000 * count + 250000`. A sixth case adds credits `CR_1` 250000 on `INV_00`, `CR_2` 125000 on `INV_01`, `CR_3` 125000 on `INV_01`, with payment 500000. 18 cases.

**`sum_of_others`.** `amounts` starts as `[10000, 10000]` and each next element is the sum of all before it, until there are 11. For `count` in `(5, 8, 11)`: invoices `INV_00` onward with the first `count` amounts, and four payments: the last amount; the last amount plus the first; the sum of all `count`; `amounts[2] + 1`. Then one four-invoice chain `INV_A` 600000, `INV_B` 400000, `INV_C` 1000000, `INV_D` 2000000 with payments 1000000 and 2000000. 14 cases.

**`credits_equal_to_differences`.** `base = 100000`. For `count` in `(3, 5, 7)`: invoices `INV_BASE_1` and `INV_BASE_2` of `base`, plus for `index` in `range(count)` an invoice `INV_X<index>` of `base + 10000 * (index + 1)` with a credit `CR_X<index>` of `10000 * (index + 1)` on it. Four payments: `base`, `2 * base`, `base + 10000`, `3 * base + 20000`. Then two single cases. Two customers: `INV_A` 120000 of `CUS_1` with `CR_1` 20000 on it, `INV_B` 100000 of `CUS_2`, payment 100000. Zero net: `INV_A` 100000, `INV_Z` 30000 with `CR_Z` 30000 on it, payment 100000. 14 cases.

**`one_minor_unit_apart`.** `base = 100000`. For `count` in `(5, 9)`: invoices `INV_00` onward with amounts `base + index - count // 2`. Credits `CR_1` of 1 cent on `INV_00`, `CR_2` of 1 cent on `INV_01`, `CR_3` of 2 cents on `INV_01`. For each payment in `(base, base - 1, base + 1, 2 * base, 2 * base + 1, 3 * base - 2, base * count)`: one case without the credits and one with them. 28 cases.

### 8.3 Cost

The whole file ran in about 10 seconds on Python 3.9.25 and about 9 seconds on 3.13.18 in the dry run.

The large ledgers are kept at 17 to 20 invoices on purpose. On ledgers of 18 to 40 invoices per customer CP-SAT itself needed up to five seconds per ledger and was cut off in 17 of 120 at a deterministic limit of 2.0. A test that depends on a cut-off would pin CP-SAT's search behaviour, which may differ between platforms. At 17 to 20 invoices no limit is needed.
