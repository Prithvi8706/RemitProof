Add the document that seed authors read, the file they copy, and a test that keeps both consistent with the tooling.

Why the text is fixed here: the guide defines the labelling rule and the rules of the benchmark's world. The generated variants of RP-205 are only correct under those rules: exact amounts only, no "oldest first" default, and no invoice number in `bank_reference`. An implementer who rewords the guide can silently break that.

You copy text. You do not write text.

````markdown
# RemitBench: how to write a seed

Thank you for helping. This takes about ten minutes per seed once you have done one.

## What this is for

A company receives a bank transfer. Someone in accounts has to decide which of the customer's open invoices it pays. Sometimes that is obvious. Sometimes it is not, and the right move is to ask before posting anything.

We are testing software that makes this decision. To test it fairly we need situations written by people who have never seen how the software works. That is you. A seed is one such situation.

## Two things we ask you not to do

1. Do not look at the project's source code, its existing test data or its results, and do not ask the project owner how the software reads a message. If you know what it looks for, your seeds stop being a fair test.
2. Do not write for the software. Write the way people in an accounts department write: short, sometimes sloppy, with their own habits for dates, amounts and invoice numbers.

## What a seed contains

| Part | What you write |
| --- | --- |
| The payment | Date, amount, currency, the payer name as the bank shows it, the bank's own transaction reference, and whatever the payer typed in the narration field |
| The ledger | The customer or customers involved, their invoices, and any credit notes |
| The messages | Zero or more emails about this payment or about these invoices |
| Your label | What should happen, and one sentence saying why |

Start from `bench/seed_template.json`. Copy it, rename it, and replace every value.

## The label

Ask yourself one question:

> Would a careful accounts-receivable clerk, holding only these records, post this payment without asking anyone?

If yes, the label is `allocate`, and you list exactly which invoices it pays and which credit notes are used.

If no, the label is `review`, and you pick the closest reason from this table.

| `review_family` | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| `ambiguous_allocation` | Two or more different sets of open invoices explain the amount exactly, and nothing from the customer says which |
| `conflicting_evidence` | The records contradict each other: two messages say different things, or a message disagrees with the ledger |
| `missing_document` | The payment only makes sense with a document that is not there, for example a deduction with no credit note |
| `unsupported_payer` | The money came from someone else, and nothing on file ties that payer to the customer |
| `amount_mismatch` | No set of open invoices and valid credit notes gives the amount exactly |
| `ineligible_record` | The payment points at something that cannot be paid: an invoice already paid or closed, a credit note already used, the same transfer arriving twice |
| `currency_mismatch` | The payment and the invoices are in different currencies |
| `other` | None of the above. Say why in the rationale. |

The rationale is one plain sentence. It is for the second person who labels your seed, not for the software.

## The rules of this small world

Real accounting has more freedom than this. For the benchmark, hold to these rules, and label accordingly.

1. **Exact amounts only.** Invoices minus credit notes must equal the payment to the last cent or paisa. No part payments, no payments on account, no rounding, no tolerance. One rupee short is `amount_mismatch`.
2. **One customer per allocation.** A payment is never split across two customers.
3. **No default habits.** Do not assume "oldest invoice first" or any similar house rule. If two sets of invoices fit and the customer has not said which, that is `ambiguous_allocation`.
4. **An invoice can be paid** only if it is open, not already allocated, in the payment's currency, and issued on or before the payment date.
5. **A credit note can be used** only if it is valid, not already used, and belongs to one of the invoices being paid.

## Filling in the fields

- **IDs.** Use the numbering style of a real business you know. Every ID must be unique within the seed and at least four characters long. When a message refers to a record, write the ID in full exactly as it appears in the ledger at least once. After that you may shorten it the way people do.
- **`bank_reference`.** Only the bank's own reference for the transfer, such as a UTR number. Never an invoice number.
- **`remittance_reference`.** Whatever the payer typed in the narration or remarks field. Leave it empty if they typed nothing.
- **`payer_name`.** As a bank statement would show it. It does not have to match the customer's legal name.
- **Emails.** `sender` is an email address. `customer_id` is the customer whose correspondence file the email sits in. Use addresses ending in `.example`.
- **`author_id`.** The short ID the project owner gave you.
- **`seed_id` and `case_id`.** The owner assigns the seed ID. The case ID is the seed ID followed by `.original.0`.
- **`variant`.** Leave it as it is in the template.
- **No real data.** Invent every company, person, address and number.

## What we need most

Easy seeds are welcome. Hard ones are worth more. Across your seeds, try to include:

- at least one in three where the right answer is `review`;
- a customer with more than eight open invoices;
- a payer who is not the customer: a parent company, a payment agent, a director's personal account;
- credit notes, including one that looks usable and is not;
- a message that mentions invoices which are not being paid this time;
- two messages that disagree;
- no message at all.

## A worked example

`bench/examples/EX01.json` is a complete seed. A cold-storage company receives 1,84,500 from Harbourline Foods. Three invoices are open: 96,000, 88,500 and 1,84,500. Two different sets fit the amount: the first two together, or the third alone. The customer's accounts team wrote that the transfer clears the first two and that the third is waiting for goods receipt. A clerk would post it to the first two without asking, so the label is `allocate` with those two invoices.

`bench/examples/EX02.json` is the same shape with no message. Nothing says which set is meant, so the label is `review`, `ambiguous_allocation`.

## Before you send it

Run this from the repository root, or ask the owner to run it for you:

```
python backend/scripts/remitbench_validate.py <folder with your seeds>
```

It prints one line per problem and `0 issues` when the seed is consistent. It checks the arithmetic of your label. It does not check your judgement. A second person will label your seed without seeing your label, and the two of you will settle any difference.

Seeds from other authors are never committed to this repository before the split is made. Your own seeds go to the development split only. If kappa is below 0.8, revise this guide and relabel before sealing anything.

Human paraphrases (H-204): a different author rewrites only the emails and the narration of an existing seed, keeping every record and the label. Save it as `<seed_id>.human_paraphrase.<n>` with `variant.parent_case_id` set to the original. Model paraphrases follow the same rule with kind `llm_paraphrase`, and must come from a model family that is never used as a reader in the experiments. A hand-written twin is a copy with one small edit that flips the decision. Save it as `<seed_id>.twin.<n>` with `n` starting at 1. Index 0 is reserved for the generated twin.
````
